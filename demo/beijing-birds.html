Birds of Beijing: Our Wild Neighbors

Look closer. The city is full of wings, routes, and quiet signs of health.

Beijing sits on a major bird migration route, so millions of birds live in or pass through the city each year. They control pests, spread seeds, and signal whether our air, water, and green spaces are healthy. Protecting birds means protecting the city we share.

Species, homes, and threats

Beijing's birds depend on wetlands, woodlands, and even urban structures. Habitat loss, pollution, climate shifts, and human disturbance are making survival harder.

Cranes · swans · egrets

Wetlands and reservoirs give these birds food, shelter, and a safe stop during migration.

Beijing swift

Old buildings and open city skies are part of this fast-moving urban bird's home.

Eagles · warblers

Mountain forests provide nesting sites, insects, and the cover small birds need.

What gets in the way

Wetland loss, light pollution, pesticides, and illegal trapping all add pressure.

Small actions, real impact

Protect birds, together

Bird protection starts with everyday choices. Cleaner habitats, quieter green spaces, and safer buildings all help birds survive and thrive. Residents, students, and visitors can all make a difference.

  • Do not feed, chase, or handle wild birds
  • Keep cats indoors or supervised outdoors
  • Reduce plastic, pesticides, and light pollution
  • Join birdwatching, cleanups, and citizen science
  • Report injured birds or illegal trapping
